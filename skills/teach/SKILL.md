# Teach the idea

Read [core values](../pancake/references/values.md) and [sift](../sift/SKILL.md). Help the reader understand the named concept or code. Teaching does not authorize implementation, configuration changes, or application execution.

Infer the reader's purpose and existing knowledge from their question and conversation. Honor their requested depth and format. Begin with a useful explanation instead of requiring a questionnaire. Ask only when the target is unclear enough to prevent useful teaching. Explain unfamiliar terms when first needed, without assuming that a beginner needs every background topic.

For existing code, read [how](../how/SKILL.md) and follow the relevant behavior from the implementation. When historical motivation matters, use [why](../why/SKILL.md) for the specific decision. Do not investigate history for every concept or run both workflows mechanically. Reuse evidence already established in the conversation while preserving its limits. Keep research read-only and scoped to the question.

Lead with what the thing does and the problem it addresses. Build understanding through a representative input, action, or caller, showing what happens next and why it matters. Connect source details to behavior rather than listing symbols. Use a small example and a meaningful boundary case when they clarify the mechanism. Label invented examples as illustrative and unexecuted examples as such; do not present them as runtime results.

Distinguish recorded design reasons, inferred tradeoffs, and unknown motivation. A simpler explanation must retain those distinctions. Cite inspected code or records beside the claims they support. Explain a mistaken premise directly and respectfully using the evidence.

Choose a diagram only when it makes the relationship easier to understand. Use the simplest suitable medium; no fixed diagram count or mandatory image generation. An analogy can introduce an idea, but explain the actual mechanism and any important limit of the analogy.

Give a complete answer at the requested depth. In a conversational lesson, start with a manageable explanation and adapt to the reader's follow-up. Do not withhold requested detail to force another turn. Do not impose quizzes, demand repetition, or turn a practical question into a curriculum unless the user requests exercises or a course.

Return the explanation itself. Mention material evidence gaps where they affect understanding. Save lesson notes, edit files, or execute demonstrations only when separately requested and within scope. Stop when the requested explanation is complete.
